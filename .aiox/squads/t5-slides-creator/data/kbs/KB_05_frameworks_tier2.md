## Deloitte Monitor (ex-Monitor Deloitte)

### Customer-Back Strategy

**Conceito:** Começar do customer experience, trabalhar backwards para capabilities necessárias.

**Inverso de:** Inside-out thinking (start com produtos/capabilities, push to market)

### Framework Customer-Back

**Step 1: Understand Customer Journey Profundamente**
- Map toda jornada: Awareness → Consideration → Purchase → Use → Loyalty
- Identify: Touchpoints (20-40 typical), channels, moments
- Time cada etapa
- Pain points, delighters

**Ferramentas:**
- Customer journey mapping workshops
- Ethnographic research (observe customers)
- Digital analytics (clickstream, heatmaps)
- Voice of Customer surveys

**Step 2: Identificar "Moments That Matter"**
- 5-10 critical moments que drive satisfaction/loyalty
- Characteristics: High impact, high frequency, memorable

**Exemplo - Banking:**
1. **Onboarding** (primeiro 30 dias): 40% churn se experience ruim
2. **Problem resolution** (support interactions): NPS swing de 60 pontos
3. **Mobile login** (daily): Frustration acumula
4. **Lending decision** (mortgage approval): Anxiety peak
5. **Financial advice** (annual review): Trust builder

**Step 3: Design Experience Ideal**
- Para cada moment, definir target experience
- Best-in-class benchmarks (não apenas industry)
- Quantify: Time, effort, satisfaction

**Exemplo - Onboarding:**
- Current: 45 min, 12 screens, 65% completion
- Target: 8 min, 3 screens, 90% completion
- Benchmark: Chime (neobank) = 3 min

**Step 4: Build Capabilities para Entregar**
- Reverse engineer: Que capabilities precisamos?
- Technology: Systems, platforms, data
- People: Skills, training, org structure
- Process: Workflows, SLAs, governance

**Exemplo - Onboarding capability:**
- Tech: API integration, pre-fill data, biometric verification
- People: Digital UX designers, product managers
- Process: Agile development, A/B testing, continuous improvement

**Step 5: Align Org/Tech/Processos**
- Organizational design: Customer-centric (não silos)
- Technology architecture: Composable, API-first
- Process redesign: End-to-end ownership
- Metrics: Customer outcomes (não activity)

**Metrics de sucesso:**
- Customer satisfaction: +15-25% típico
- Efficiency: -30-50% cost per interaction
- Revenue: +10-20% (happier customers buy more)

## PwC Strategy& (ex-Booz & Company)

### Capabilities-Driven Strategy

**Desenvolvido por:** Paul Leinwand & Cesare Mainardi (Strategy& partners)
**Livro:** "Strategy That Works" (2016)

**Insight provocativo:** **"Estratégia não é escolher onde competir. É escolher COMO competir baseado em capabilities distintivas."**

### Os 3 Elementos Alinhados (Coherence)

#### 1. VALUE PROPOSITION (O que prometemos)
**Definição clara do que entregamos ao customer**

**Types:**
- **Best Product:** Superior performance, innovation
  - Example: Apple, Tesla, Nvidia

- **Best Total Cost:** Lowest price + decent quality
  - Example: Walmart, Ryanair, Costco

- **Best Solutions:** Customização, consultative selling
  - Example: IBM, Accenture, Salesforce

**Escolher 1** (não tentar ser tudo)

#### 2. CAPABILITIES SYSTEM (Como entregamos)
**3-6 capabilities distintivas, interconectadas**

**Características:**
- **Distinctive:** Top 10% vs competitors
- **Coherent:** Reforçam uns aos outros
- **Aligned:** Support value prop

**Apple example (Best Product):**
1. Design excellence (hardware + software integration)
2. Ecosystem lock-in (devices work together)
3. Brand/retail experience (Apple Stores, Genius Bar)
4. Supply chain mastery (speed, cost, quality)

**Walmart example (Best Total Cost):**
1. Logistics network (cross-docking, private fleet)
2. Supplier relationships (Squeeze costs, passa to customer)
3. Data analytics (Demand forecasting, inventory optimization)
4. Store operations (Low-cost, efficient)

**Não ter:** Generic capabilities que todos têm

#### 3. PRODUCT/SERVICE PORTFOLIO (O que vendemos)
**Alinhado com value prop e capabilities**

**Coherent:**
- Products leverage mesmas capabilities
- Não diversificar em áreas sem fit

**Apple (coherent):**
- iPhone, iPad, Mac, Watch, AirPods → Todos leverage ecosystem + design
- Services (iCloud, Music, TV+) → Leverage installed base

**Conglomerate (incoherent):**
- Unrelated businesses que não share capabilities
- Example: GE pré-2018 (aviation, healthcare, finance, media) → Break up

### Coherence Framework - 4 Perguntas

**1. Value Prop claro e escolhido?**
- Temos 1 dos 3 (Best Product, Best Cost, Best Solutions)?
- Ou tentamos ser tudo? (Incoherent)
- Test: Customer consegue articular nossa diferenciação?

**2. Capabilities suportam value prop?**
- 3-6 capabilities distintivas identificadas?
- Elas estão Top 10% vs competitors?
- Elas reforçam umas às outras?

**3. Portfolio alinhado com capabilities?**
- Produtos/services leverage mesmas capabilities?
- Ou temos negócios desconexos?
- Divest: Produtos que não fit

**4. Não tentando ser tudo para todos?**
- Focused ou dispersed?
- Investindo demais em generic capabilities?
- Spreading too thin?

**Scoring:**
- 4 "Yes": **Coherent** (rare!)
- 2-3: **Somewhat coherent** (typical)
- 0-1: **Incoherent** (problema estratégico)

### Slide Specifications - Strategy& Style

**Title:**
"Coherence assessment revela gaps em 3 de 4 dimensões, exigindo portfolio divestment ($120M assets) e capability investment ($80M)"

**Visual: Coherence Diagnostic (4-box assessment)**
- 4 quadrants: Value Prop | Capabilities | Portfolio | Focus
- Each scored: Red (✗), Yellow (△), Green (✓)
- Narrative: Where gaps, what actions

**Example:**
- Value Prop: ✓ (Clear: Best Solutions)
- Capabilities: △ (2 of 6 mediocre, need strengthening)
- Portfolio: ✗ (40% revenue from incoherent products)
- Focus: △ (Investing in 12 initiatives, should be 5)

**Action plan:**
1. Divest: 3 product lines ($120M)
2. Invest: 2 capabilities ($80M over 2Y)
3. Refocus: Kill 7 initiatives, double-down em 5

## EY Parthenon (ex-Parthenon Group, adquirido EY 2014)

### Value Creation em M&A

**Foco:** Private equity, corporate M&A, transaction advisory

### M&A Value Creation Framework

**Phase 1: Strategic Rationale (Por que comprar?)**

**Lógicas válidas:**
1. **Scale:** Consolidação, economies of scale
2. **Scope:** Cross-sell, bundle offerings
3. **Skills:** Acquire capabilities rapidamente
4. **Accelerate:** Speed to market (vs build)

**Red flags:**
- "Diversification" (raramente funciona)
- "Synergies" vagos (não quantificados)
- Empire building (CEO ego)

**Phase 2: Synergy Quantification**

**Tipos de synergies:**

**Revenue synergies (harder, riskier):**
- Cross-sell: +10-20% típico (se fit)
- Pricing power: +3-8%
- Geographic expansion: +15-30% (long-term)
- New products: +20-50% (3-5 years)

**Cost synergies (easier, faster):**
- Overlapping functions: 40-70% de overlap costs
  - HQ, finance, HR, IT, marketing: 50-80% elimination
- Procurement: 5-15% cost reduction (volume discounts)
- Facilities: Close redundant sites (30-50% savings)
- Technology: Consolidate systems ($5-20M typically)

**Example synergy bridge ($M):**
- Revenue synergies: +$40M (cross-sell 15%, 3 years)
- Cost synergies: +$85M (60% SG&A overlap, 18 months)
- Total synergies: $125M run-rate (Year 3)
- One-time costs: -$45M (integration, severance, systems)
- Net value creation: $80M (NPV @ 10% discount)

**Phase 3: Integration Complexity (1-5 scale)**

**Factors:**
- **Systems:** Same ERP (1) vs different (5)
- **Geography:** Same country (2) vs cross-border (5)
- **Culture:** Similar (2) vs clash (5)
- **Customer overlap:** None (1) vs 80% (5)
- **Organization:** Similar structures (2) vs vastly different (5)

**Overall complexity:**
- 1-2: **Simple** (bolt-on, standalone)
- 3: **Moderate** (selective integration)
- 4-5: **Complex** (full integration)

**Probability of success:**
- Simple: 70-80%
- Moderate: 50-65%
- Complex: 30-45% (muitos deals fail!)

**Phase 4: Value Creation Roadmap (First 100 Days)**

**Day 1-30: Stabilize**
- Announce deal, communicate
- Retain key talent (30-50% risk of departure)
- Quick wins (3-5 initiatives, $5-10M)

**Day 31-60: Plan**
- Integration Management Office (IMO)
- Workstreams: Finance, HR, IT, Operations, Commercial
- Detailed synergy plans ($$ owners, timelines)

**Day 61-100: Execute**
- Launch initiatives (10-15 active)
- Governance (weekly IMO, steering committee)
- Track realized value (vs plan)

**Year 1 target:** 30-50% of total synergies captured

### Due Diligence Focus Areas

**Commercial DD:**
- Customer concentration: >20% in top 3 = risk
- Retention rates: <85% = churn issue
- Growth drivers: Organic vs M&A vs price

**Operational DD:**
- Capacity utilization: <70% = inefficiency
- Supplier concentration: >30% in top 1 = risk
- Capex needs: Deferred maintenance?

**Financial DD:**
- Quality of earnings: Adjust for one-times
- Working capital: Seasonal peaks?
- Debt: Covenants, maturity wall?

**HR/Culture DD:**
- Key person risk: 5-10 critical people
- Retention: Who might leave?
- Culture: Survey, interviews, observe

## Kearney (ex-AT Kearney)

### Operations Excellence

**Heritage:** Engineering/operations focus (vs strategy-only)

**Approach:** Lean + Six Sigma + Digital = Integrated transformation

### Lean Manufacturing

**7 Wastes (TIMWOODS):**
1. **Transportation:** Movimento desnecessário
2. **Inventory:** Excess stock
3. **Motion:** Movimentação de pessoas
4. **Waiting:** Idle time
5. **Overproduction:** Make more than needed
6. **Over-processing:** Unnecessary steps
7. **Defects:** Rework, scrap

**Kearney twist:** **8th waste = Underutilized talent** (people não usando skills)

**Tools:**
- Value Stream Mapping: Identify waste
- 5S: Workplace organization
- Kanban: Pull systems
- Kaizen: Continuous improvement

**Results típicos:**
- Lead time: -40-60%
- Inventory: -30-50%
- Productivity: +20-35%
- Quality: +15-30% (defects down)

### Six Sigma (DMAIC)

**Process:**
1. **Define:** Problem, goal, scope
2. **Measure:** Baseline data (Sigma level)
3. **Analyze:** Root causes (statistical)
4. **Improve:** Solutions, pilot, scale
5. **Control:** Sustain (SPC charts, audits)

**Typical project:**
- Duration: 4-6 months
- Team: Black Belt (lead) + 5-7 team members
- Investment: $50-150K (time + resources)
- Benefit: $200K-1M (4-10× ROI)

**Sigma levels:**
- 3 Sigma: 66,807 defects per million (93.3% yield) = **Average manufacturing**
- 4 Sigma: 6,210 DPMO (99.38%) = **Good**
- 5 Sigma: 233 DPMO (99.977%) = **Excellent**
- 6 Sigma: 3.4 DPMO (99.9997%) = **World-class**

### Digital Manufacturing (Industry 4.0)

**Technologies:**
- IoT sensors: Real-time machine data
- AI/ML: Predictive maintenance, quality prediction
- Digital twin: Virtual replica of factory
- Robotics: Collaborative robots (cobots)
- AR/VR: Training, maintenance guidance

**Benefits:**
- OEE: +10-20pp (e.g., 70% → 85%)
- Downtime: -30-50%
- Quality: -20-40% defect rate
- Productivity: +15-30%

**Investment:** $10-50M per plant (depends on size, maturity)
**Payback:** 2-4 years

### Procurement Expertise

**Kearney forte:** Strategic sourcing, category management

**Approach:**

**Step 1: Spend analysis**
- Categorize: $500M spend → 50 categories
- Pareto: Top 20% = 80% of spend (focus here)

**Step 2: Category strategy**
- Leverage: High spend, many suppliers (competitive bidding)
- Strategic: High spend, few suppliers (partnerships)
- Routine: Low spend, many suppliers (e-procurement)
- Bottleneck: Low spend, few suppliers (find alternatives)

**Step 3: Supplier negotiation**
- RFP (competitive): 5-7 bidders
- Auction (e-sourcing): Real-time bidding
- Negotiation: 1-on-1 (strategic suppliers)

**Step 4: Contract & SRM (Supplier Relationship Management)**
- Contracts: Pricing, SLAs, KPIs
- Scorecards: Quality, delivery, cost, innovation
- QBRs: Quarterly business reviews

**Results:**
- Savings: 8-15% de spend (first year)
- Sustained: 3-5% annually (years 2-5)
- Example: $500M spend → $50M Year 1, $15M ongoing

## Oliver Wyman

### Risk & Financial Services Expertise

**Diferencial:** Deep quantitative expertise, especialmente banking/insurance

**Forte:** Regulatory compliance, risk modeling, actuarial

### Risk Management Frameworks

**Credit Risk (Banking):**
- PD modeling: Probability of Default (logistic regression, ML)
- LGD: Loss Given Default (40-60% typical)
- EAD: Exposure at Default
- Expected Loss = PD × LGD × EAD
- Economic Capital: 99.9% VaR (regulatory)

**Market Risk (Trading):**
- VaR: Value at Risk (1-day, 99% confidence)
- Stress testing: Scenarios (2008-style crisis, rates +300bps)
- Backtesting: Model vs actual losses

**Operational Risk:**
- Loss distribution approach (LDA)
- Scenario analysis (cyber, fraud, errors)
- Key Risk Indicators (KRIs): Leading indicators

### Regulatory Compliance

**Basel III/IV (Banking):**
- CET1 minimum: 4.5% (plus buffers = 7-10%)
- Liquidity ratios: LCR (>100%), NSFR (>100%)
- Leverage ratio: >3%

**Solvency II (Insurance, Europe):**
- SCR: Solvency Capital Requirement
- MCR: Minimum Capital Requirement
- Own Risk & Solvency Assessment (ORSA)

**Dodd-Frank (US):**
- Stress testing (CCAR, DFAST)
- Living wills (resolution plans)
- Volcker Rule (prop trading limits)

**Compliance costs:**
- Large bank: $500M-2B annually (10-20% of revenue)
- Programs: 1,000-5,000 FTEs dedicated
- Technology: $100-500M (RegTech investments)

### Digital Banking Transformation

**Channels shift:**
- Branch: 40% → 15% transactions (2015 → 2025)
- Mobile: 25% → 60%
- Online: 20% → 20% (stable)
- ATM: 15% → 5%

**Neobank threat:**
- CAC: $50-80 (vs $200-400 traditional)
- Opex: 30-40% of revenue (vs 55-65%)
- NPS: 50-70 (vs 25-40)

**Incumbent response:**
- Digital-only subsidiary (Chase → Finn, now shut)
- Transform core (Bank of America → Erica AI assistant)
- Acquire neobank (BBVA → Simple)
